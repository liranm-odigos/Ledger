The morning was clear, and the road into town was already dry. People carried baskets and talked about ordinary things: bread, the price of apples, a letter that had come late. In the square a clerk sat with a book open and wrote down what each person brought. The work was slow and plain. A neighbor stopped to ask about the bridge. The answer was short, and then both of them moved on. By noon the list was longer, the shade had shifted, and someone had left a cup of water on the step. None of this would be remembered for its drama. It would be remembered, if at all, because somebody had taken the trouble to write it in common words while the day was still happening. Later a reader could walk back through those lines and know who came, what they carried, and when they left. The cup was still there in the evening, and the clerk closed the book. A cart passed, the dust settled, and the square grew quiet again as the light thinned. The square stayed quiet the rest of the night.
